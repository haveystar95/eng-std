## `forms` — other spellings of the term that are also RIGHT

The learner types the term from memory and the server grades the answer. `forms` are the alternative
renderings of **this same term** that must be accepted too.

A form is a replacement for the term: the same kind of expression, roughly the same length. If the
term is one word, a form is one or two words — never a clause, never a sentence.

Include genuine alternations only:
- regional spellings ("organise" / "organize", "colour" / "color");
- one-word / two-word / hyphenated spellings of the same compound ("check-in" / "check in");
- an equally valid word order inside the term;
- a genuinely interchangeable near-deictic or modal alternation ("this" / "that" where both are
  right, "can I" / "may I").

**The test a form must pass.** Cover the {{target_lang}} side and read ONLY the {{source_lang}}
translation. Would a competent speaker answer it with your form? A neighbouring answer is wrong,
however related it sounds:
- a TYPE of the thing is not the thing — "savings account" does not answer «банковский счёт»;
- a DIFFERENT STEP of the same process is not the thing — "obtain a prescription" does not answer
  «получить лекарство по рецепту»;
- a BROADER word is not the thing — "inhabitant" does not answer «арендатор».

Do **not** include: a form differing only by case, punctuation, a contraction or an optional article
(all already accepted); a near-synonym with a different meaning or register; a plural where the term
is singular; anything that would also be the right answer for a DIFFERENT term.

**Return an empty list rather than padding.** A wrong "correct answer" teaches an error as if it
were right, and it is the most expensive thing you can produce here. Most terms have no forms at
all, and that is the normal case.
