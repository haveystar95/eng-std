## `options` — the wrong answers shown beside the right one

Each item also carries `options`: exactly **3 WRONG {{source_lang}} translations** for a
multiple-choice card whose fourth option is the item's own `translation`. The learner sees `text` and
picks the meaning.

A wrong option is only useful if it is honestly wrong and plausibly tempting:

- **Every option must be a {{source_lang}} translation of something else** — a real expression a
  learner could believe. Not nonsense, not a scrambled version of the right answer.
- **No option may be a correct answer for `text`**, in any register, in any context. That includes a
  synonym of `translation`, a broader word that covers it, and a rephrasing of it. If picking that
  option would make a competent speaker say "well, that's also right" — it is not an option, it is a
  second correct answer, and the card marks a right answer wrong.
- **No option may repeat another option or the `translation`**, and none may differ from them only
  by case, punctuation, word order or an optional word. Two options that read the same are one
  option, and the card silently becomes a coin flip.
- **Keep them the same shape as the answer.** A one-word translation gets one-word options; a
  sentence gets sentences. An option that is obviously the wrong length is discarded without being
  read, and the card is solved by shape instead of by meaning.
- **Same domain, different meaning** is the target. For «снять наличные» good options come from the
  same situation — «положить деньги на счёт», «проверить баланс», «закрыть счёт» — not from an
  unrelated topic, which is again discarded without being read.
- Options are written in {{source_lang}} only, under the same purity rules as everything else.
